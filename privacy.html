PHASER

Privacy

Phaser is software from Keramas, Inc. that medical device companies use to draft and check the documents in their Design History Files. This page says what Phaser receives, what it does with it, and how to reach us.

Who we are

Keramas, Inc. makes and runs Phaser. For anything about your data, write to info@keramas.ai.

What Phaser receives when you sign in

You sign in with your company's Google Workspace account. Google sends Phaser your name, your email address and your company's domain, and confirms that the address is verified. Phaser keeps those three things so it knows who you are and which company you belong to. Phaser never sees or stores your password, and it asks Google for nothing else: no mail, no calendar, no files.

Phaser's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

What your company puts in

People at your company upload documents and enter facts about their projects. That content belongs to your company. Phaser uses it for one thing: to draft your company's own documents and to report how far each one covers the procedures and standards it answers to. Keramas staff open a company's Phaser only to help that company, and every such access is recorded.

Where it is processed

Phaser runs on Amazon Web Services in the United States (us-west-2, Oregon) under Keramas's own account. AI drafting runs on Amazon Bedrock, where Keramas's account is set to keep nothing: the model providers do not store your content and do not train on it. Keramas does not sell your data, does not share it with anyone else, and does not use it to train anything.

Cookies

Phaser sets two cookies: one that keeps you signed in, and one that lasts only for the moment of the sign-in handshake. There are no analytics, no tracking scripts and no third-party cookies.

How long we keep it

For as long as your company uses Phaser. Your company can ask for its content to be deleted by writing to info@keramas.ai, and Keramas will confirm when it is done. One thing stays: the record of which administrator gave or removed each person's access, kept for security review.
